SORAA'27 The 1st International Workshop on Software Organizations in the Era of AI Agents

AI agents are becoming active participants in software engineering, changing not only how software is developed but also how teams coordinate, distribute responsibilities, manage knowledge, and ensure quality, security, trust, and accountability.

SORAA'27 brings researchers and practitioners together to study the organizational, technical, and socio-technical implications of human-agent software engineering.

About SORAA'27

Large language models, generative AI, and autonomous agents are taking on a growing share of software engineering work, across development, testing, security, DevOps/MLOps, deployment, and maintenance.

The key research question is no longer only whether AI improves individual tasks. We also need to understand how AI agents change software organizations: their team structures, developer roles, work allocation, coordination, knowledge sharing, architecture, code ownership, governance, trust, security, and accountability.

SORAA'27 establishes an interdisciplinary forum connecting researchers and practitioners in AI for software engineering, HCI and human-AI collaboration, empirical software engineering, software architecture, testing, performance, security, and DevOps/MLOps.

Workshop goals

  1. G1

    Understand how agents reshape team structures, human-agent collaboration, expertise, and work allocation.

  2. G2

    Engineer effective agent-supported workflows across the software lifecycle.

  3. G3

    Measure organizational and engineering impacts using empirical methods, metrics, and benchmarks.

  4. G4

    Govern agent autonomy through oversight, accountability, security, and trust.

  5. G5

    Connect academic and industrial communities to identify shared challenges and research directions.

Topics of Interest

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to, the following themes.

  1. 1

    Human–Agent Teams and Collaboration

    Team structures, developer roles, delegation, expertise, knowledge sharing, and human oversight.

  2. 2

    Agentic Software Development and Evolution

    AI-generated changes, code ownership, maintenance, refactoring, and long-term software quality.

  3. 3

    Agentic DevOps, MLOps, and Operations

    CI/CD, deployment, monitoring, incident management, and autonomous operational workflows.

  4. 4

    Testing, Quality, and Performance Engineering

    Autonomous testing, performance assessment, reliability, and human-agent quality assurance.

  5. 5

    Security, Trust, and Responsible Engineering

    Agent permissions, secure workflows, accountability, risk management, and governance.

  6. 6

    Architecture and Socio-Technical Alignment

    Architectural decisions, organizational coupling, coordination dependencies, and architecture evolution.

  7. 7

    Multi-Agent Engineering and Coordination

    Agent specialization, orchestration, communication, conflict resolution, and collaborative workflows.

  8. 8

    Empirical Studies and Industrial Experiences

    Organizational metrics, repository mining, benchmarks, adoption challenges, and lessons learned.

SORAA'27 particularly welcomes industrial experience reports, case studies, tool demonstrations, vision papers, empirical studies, and negative results.

Contributions should examine how AI agents affect organizations, coordination, responsibilities, engineering workflows, or governance, rather than focusing solely on task-level automation.

Call for Contributions

SORAA'27 invites researchers and practitioners to submit contributions examining how AI agents are changing software organizations and engineering practices.

We explicitly welcome:

  • Early-stage research
  • Industrial experiences
  • Negative results
  • Interdisciplinary work

Submission categories

  • Research Extended Abstract

    3–5 pages

    Preliminary empirical findings, methods, models, measurements, or technical approaches related to software organizations and AI agents.

  • Vision and Position Extended Abstract

    2–4 pages

    Emerging challenges, conceptual frameworks, provocative positions, research directions, or new organizational models.

  • Industrial Experience Extended Abstract

    2–5 pages

    Real-world AI-agent adoption, lessons learned, organizational impacts, deployment experiences, challenges, or unsuccessful practices.

  • Tool and Demonstration Extended Abstract

    2–4 pages

    Prototypes, datasets, benchmarks, analysis tools, agent-based engineering workflows, or demonstrations.

Review process

Each submission will receive at least two independent reviews. Reviewers will consider:

  • Relevance
  • Originality
  • Clarity
  • Methodological soundness, where applicable
  • Practical significance
  • Potential to stimulate workshop discussion

Negative results and experience-based contributions are explicitly encouraged.

Submission instructions and formatting details will be updated when confirmed by the organizers and FSE'27.
