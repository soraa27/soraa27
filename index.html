About

AI agents are beginning to take on work inside software organizations: writing and reviewing code, triaging issues, operating infrastructure, and coordinating with the people around them.

SORAA'27 brings together researchers and practitioners to examine what this means for how software organizations are structured, how teams collaborate, how work is divided between people and agents, and how responsibility, trust, and expertise develop when some members of a team are not human.

The workshop is co-located with the ACM International Conference on the Foundations of Software Engineering (FSE 2027). Further details about the format and schedule will be announced on this page.

Topics

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Organizational structure

    Team topologies, roles, and reporting lines when AI agents take part in software work.

  • Human–agent collaboration

    Coordination, communication, and handoffs between developers and AI agents.

  • Processes and practices

    How development, review, testing, and operations workflows change with agents in the loop.

  • Accountability and governance

    Ownership, oversight, auditability, and decision rights for agent-produced work.

  • Trust and adoption

    How individuals and organizations decide when and how far to rely on agents.

  • Skills, roles, and careers

    Changing expertise, onboarding, learning, and professional identity for software engineers.

  • Productivity and well-being

    Measuring effects on delivery, quality, workload, and developer experience.

  • Ethics and society

    Labor, fairness, and responsibility questions raised by agent-based software work.

  • Methods and evidence

    Empirical studies, industrial experience reports, and research methods for studying these organizations.
