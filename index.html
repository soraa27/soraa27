SORAA'27 The 1st International Workshop on Software Organizations in the Era of AI Agents

AI agents are becoming active participants in software engineering, changing not only how software is developed but also how teams coordinate, distribute responsibilities, manage knowledge, and ensure quality, security, trust, and accountability.

SORAA'27 brings researchers and practitioners together to study the organizational, technical, and socio-technical implications of human-agent software engineering.

About SORAA'27

The growing adoption of autonomous AI agents is transforming software engineering from human-centered development supported by AI tools toward environments where humans and agents jointly perform engineering work.

Beyond automation and productivity, this transformation raises fundamental questions about how software organizations allocate responsibilities, coordinate activities, manage knowledge, maintain code ownership, and establish governance and accountability.

SORAA'27 establishes an interdisciplinary forum dedicated to the organizational and socio-technical implications of AI agents in software engineering. Rather than treating agent capability or task performance as the principal outcome, it focuses on human–agent teams, evolving developer roles, coordination dependencies, organizational structures, knowledge sharing, architectural alignment, and governance. Software development, testing, DevOps, security, and maintenance serve as application contexts for investigating these changes.

The workshop brings together researchers and practitioners in empirical software engineering, software processes, human–computer interaction, software architecture, AI for software engineering, and industrial practice.

Workshop Goals

  1. G1

    Characterize emerging human–agent team structures, roles, and responsibility allocation.

  2. G2

    Investigate how agents reshape coordination, ownership, knowledge sharing, and socio-technical alignment across engineering workflows.

  3. G3

    Develop empirical methods, metrics, and benchmarks for evaluating organizational impacts.

  4. G4

    Examine governance, human oversight, trust, security, and accountability in agent-mediated organizations.

  5. G5

    Connect academic and industrial communities to identify practical challenges and shared research directions.

Topics of Interest

The workshop welcomes contributions organized around four complementary research themes.

  1. 1

    Human–Agent Organizations and Roles

    Human–agent team composition, delegation, developer roles, expertise distribution, decision authority, work allocation, organizational adaptation, and knowledge transfer.

  2. 2

    Coordination, Ownership, and Socio-Technical Alignment

    Human–agent and inter-agent coordination in organizational workflows, code and service ownership, communication dependencies, architectural decision-making, organizational coupling, socio-technical congruence, and software evolution.

  3. 3

    Governance, Accountability, and Organizational Trust

    Human oversight, agent autonomy and permissions, responsibility for agent-generated changes, review and approval processes, auditability, organizational security practices, and risk management.

  4. 4

    Empirical Evaluation and Industrial Transformation

    Organizational metrics, longitudinal and comparative studies, repository mining, human–agent collaboration studies, industrial adoption experiences, negative results, and benchmarks for evaluating organizational and socio-technical effects.

These themes span software development, architecture, testing, quality assurance, security, DevOps/MLOps, deployment, and maintenance. Such engineering activities are considered application contexts rather than independent workshop topics.

Contributions should explicitly examine how AI agents affect organizational structures, roles, coordination, responsibilities, knowledge, governance, or socio-technical relationships.

Work focused solely on agent architectures, prompting, model performance, or task-level automation without an organizational contribution falls outside the workshop's primary scope.

Call for Contributions

SORAA'27 invites researchers and practitioners to submit contributions examining how AI agents are changing software organizations and engineering practices.

We explicitly welcome:

  • Early-stage research
  • Empirical studies
  • Industrial experiences
  • Vision and position papers
  • Tool demonstrations
  • Negative results
  • Interdisciplinary work

Submission Categories

SORAA'27 solicits four types of contributions, all classified as extended abstracts.

  • Research Extended Abstract

    3–5 pages

    Preliminary empirical findings, methods, models, measurements, or technical approaches related to software organizations and AI agents.

  • Vision and Position Extended Abstract

    2–4 pages

    Emerging challenges, conceptual frameworks, provocative positions, research directions, or new organizational models.

  • Industrial Experience Extended Abstract

    2–5 pages

    Real-world AI-agent adoption, lessons learned, organizational impacts, deployment experiences, challenges, or unsuccessful practices.

  • Tool and Demonstration Extended Abstract

    2–4 pages

    Prototypes, datasets, benchmarks, analysis tools, agent-based engineering workflows, or demonstrations.

Review Process

Each submission will receive at least two independent reviews. Reviewers will consider:

  • Relevance
  • Originality
  • Clarity
  • Methodological soundness, where applicable
  • Practical significance
  • Potential to stimulate workshop discussion

Negative results and experience-based contributions are explicitly encouraged.

Submission instructions and formatting details will be updated when confirmed by the organizers and FSE'27.

Before submitting, please read the organizer submission policy.

Interactive Discussions

Attendees will join moderated breakout discussions addressing three questions:

  1. 1

    How should human–agent teams be structured, and how should roles and coordination mechanisms evolve?

  2. 2

    How should organizations allocate ownership, decision authority, and accountability as agents gain autonomy?

  3. 3

    Which empirical methods, metrics, datasets, and industrial evidence are needed to evaluate the organizational and socio-technical consequences of agent adoption?

Each breakout group will report its findings in a plenary discussion.

The workshop will conclude by consolidating these insights into a preliminary community research agenda, identifying emerging organizational models, open research challenges, evaluation priorities, and opportunities for academic–industrial collaboration.

Expected Community Outcomes

  • Human–Agent Organization Taxonomy

    A preliminary taxonomy of human–agent organizational structures.

  • Community Research Agenda

    Emerging organizational models, open research challenges, evaluation priorities, and opportunities for academic–industrial collaboration.

  • Empirical Evaluation Roadmap

    Methods, metrics, datasets, and industrial evidence for evaluating the organizational and socio-technical consequences of agent adoption.

The workshop will consolidate participant discussions into a publicly accessible summary of these outcomes.

Policies

Proceedings

SORAA'27 intends to publish accepted extended abstracts in the FSE'27 workshop proceedings in the ACM Digital Library, subject to FSE'27 proceedings arrangements.

All contribution types are extended abstracts of at most five pages and are therefore intended to be eligible for the article processing charge (APC) exemption specified by FSE'27.

Research Ethics

SORAA'27 will follow applicable ACM publication and research-ethics policies. The Call for Contributions will reproduce verbatim the ACM Publications Policy notification required by the FSE'27 workshop call, including the policy on research involving human participants and subjects.

ACM Publications Policy notification: Coming soon

Conflicts of Interest

Conflicts of interest will be handled according to ACM SIGSOFT policies.

Organizer Submission Policy

In accordance with SIGSOFT policy, workshop organizers and their directly supervised students, postdocs, and employees may not submit to SORAA'27.

Participation

Attendance is open to all registered workshop participants, without a separate selection procedure and regardless of whether they have an accepted contribution.

At least one author of each published contribution must register for the workshop.
